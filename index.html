Uni-VLaT: Whole-Body Tactile Adaptation of VLA Policies for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

Zihao Wang*, Shutong Liu*, Siqi Zheng, Liu Cao, Ruoqi Chen, Rundong Liu, Yanchao Yang, Mengdi Xu†

* Equal contribution · † Corresponding author

Under review at IEEE ICRA 2027

Project page

Abstract. Physical contact often determines how a humanoid should respond during loco-manipulation, yet vision and proprioception alone are often insufficient to characterize physical interaction, especially when the contact region is occluded. Unlike sparse force or torque measurements at predefined regions, distributed tactile sensing preserves spatially resolved contact patterns across the robot body. We therefore study how to integrate such whole-body tactile information into vision-language-action (VLA) policies for contact-rich control. Our approach, Uni-VLaT, introduces a tactile pathway whose latent state is trained not only for action generation, but also to predict future tactile, proprioceptive, and visual representations. This predictive objective builds a tactile-anchored multimodal context, encouraging a more structured understanding of the physical world. We evaluate Uni-VLaT on five real-robot tasks covering tactile-triggered locomotion, sustained physical interaction, human–robot contact, and loco-manipulation. Uni-VLaT achieves a 75% average success rate, outperforming a baseline without tactile input by 43 points and a tactile-input baseline without predictive supervision by 7 points. Across two pretrained VLA backbones, our method improves Table Sweeping by 30 points on both backbones and Back-Tap Walking by 85–90 points. Ablations further show that contextualized tactile prediction and absolute future targets are critical to performance. These results indicate that predictive tactile learning provides an effective route for extending pretrained VLA policies to whole-body physical interaction.

From Frozen Pose to Live Dance: Stochastic Browser Fingerprinting for Robust Risk-Based Authentication

Shutong Liu · First author

Under review at USENIX Security 2027

Abstract. Risk-Based Authentication (RBA) leverages browser fingerprinting to compare device traits from current and past sessions, helping websites detect risky logins and reduce unnecessary multi-factor authentication prompts. However, current browser fingerprinting is predominantly static and effectively operates as a one-shot snapshot, rendering it fragile to malicious replay and emulation such as phishing attacks. Attackers can replay browser fingerprints obtained from phishing to impersonate users across websites, undermining RBA. In this paper, we study the characteristics of simple content rendering and generation in Canvas, WebGL and Audio. To fundamentally improve the robustness of RBA, based on our findings, we are the first to introduce Stochastic Browser Fingerprinting (S-BFP), a novel browser fingerprinting methodology that embeds controlled randomness or “nonce” to defend against such attacks on RBA. Our key insight is that S-BFP produces controllable different browser fingerprints, even when executed on the same machine rendering identical characters and shapes, creating a unique, robust and replay-resistant device identity. We empirically demonstrate that S-BFP can effectively defend against replay and scripted emulation attacks across major browsers and hardware on more than 100 devices, maintaining stable identification for benign users with negligible overhead.

Mitigating Agentic Deception via Evaluation-Side Partially Observable Games

Shutong Liu · Second author

Under review at ICLR 2027

Abstract. As large language models become more capable agents, they can exhibit deceptive behaviors, cheating human for higher rewards while appearing helpful. We attribute this failure to evaluation-side partial observability, where models are evaluated mainly from final outcome, without checking the faithfulness of underlying reasoning, tool use, and environmental effects. Prior mitigations addressing this partial observability rely on predefined evaluator latent belief space or one-shot monitoring, limited in free-form language interactions and vulnerable to stronger deceptions that evade simple monitoring. In this work, we formulate deception mitigation as an Evaluation-Side Partially Observable Extensive-Form Game (e-PoG), a max-min framework in which an agent maximizes reward, a monitor minimizes the agent’s payoff by exposing deception from the full interaction trajectory, and a fixed verifier validates the monitor’s claims and converts them into penalties. We define the solution concept as a Subgame-Perfect Oversight Equilibrium (SPOE) between agent and monitor, and prove its existence in finite games. With a perfect verifier, every SPOE is faithful; with an imperfect verifier, the guarantee persists if verifier errors do not reverse the ranking of monitor actions. To achieve faithful SPOE, we derive a two-timescale policy gradient algorithm and enhance the pipeline empirically with NLI verification and deterministic checks. Experiments with Gemma3-4B-IT and Qwen3-4B/8B on Agentic Role-Playing, SearchQA, and WebShop show that e-PoG outperforms strong RL and monitoring baselines, mitigating multiple emergent deception modes while preserving task performance, and remains effective with weak-to-strong supervision.

Safety and Security of Satellite Communications: A Comprehensive Review of Radio-Frequency and Free-Space Optical Systems

Weizhe Liu, Shujiang Wu, Shutong Liu, Qiang Wei, Yunhong Wang

Submitted to Security and Safety

Abstract. As satellite communication (SatCom) systems expand, ensuring their safety and security becomes critical to protect against both unintentional hazards and intentional threats. We first provide an overview of radio-frequency (RF) and free-space optical (FSO) SatComs, comparing their characteristics, applications, and challenges. Subsequently, we clarify the concepts of safety and security, proposing a hierarchical taxonomy of safety and security issues specific to RF and FSO SatComs, along with their respective mitigation and countermeasures. While FSO communication is often perceived as inherently secure due to its narrow, highly directional beams, this perception has led to relatively less focus on its security aspects. Following a systematic taxonomy, we identify significant gaps in FSO security research, highlighting the need for focused investigation in this area. Finally, drawing lessons from the development of RF SatCom systems, we suggest future directions for improving the safety and security of FSO SatCom systems.
